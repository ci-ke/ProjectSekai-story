184-6 My Weapon

(Character: Ena Shinonome)

[Art Class]

Yukihira: So, you couldn't produce a failed work?
Ena: Yes. I tried numerous times, but none of them ended up as failures...
Yukihira: I see.
Yukihira: Since I've asked you to “produce a failed work”, you are required to finish and submit it.
Yukihira: If you are serious about going to art school, then please don't forget that.
Ena: I won't...
Yukihira: However, Shinonome, I feel that you've come to an important realization regarding this assignment.
Ena: Huh...?
Yukihira: Do you recall mentioning the other day that you felt compelled to draw something which would do well in the contest?
Ena: Oh... Y-Yes, I remember.
Ena: I felt that I had to use everything at my disposal since I'm not nearly as talented as the others.
Yukihira: I won't say drawing with that mindset is wrong. It's a reasonable one to have.
Yukihira: Getting into Tokyo Art University isn't a pretty process. Primarily because you need to overwhelm your peers with your artistic skills and charm.
Yukihira: However, that sort of thinking can also make your art less appealing.
Ena: Huh?
Yukihira: What do you think your best weapon is, Shinonome?
Ena: Umm... M-My composition skills?
Yukihira: It's your artistic style.
Yukihira: Whenever you're drawing something that you genuinely wish to, you create these compelling worlds which only you can.
Yukihira: However, when you feel that you have to win or not fail, you quickly lose your grasp on that ability.
Yukihira: As you put more and more pressure on yourself, you begin to seek the correct answer and fear making mistakes far too much. The resulting work is something bland.
Ena: Oh...
Yukihira: The goal of having you produce a failed work this time around is to free you from those restraints.
Yukihira: By forcing yourself to think that you have to fail, you naturally become more inclined to express yourself in ways which you normally wouldn't.
Ena: Come to think of it, I did try drawing in ways I usually don't in order to fail...
Yukihira: The technical aspects of visual art are based upon robust principles.
Yukihira: Why a piece of art is considered technically sound or the opposite can be described by those principles.
Ena: Right...
Yukihira: However, that being said...
Yukihira: Theory alone is not enough to tell you the “correct” way to draw something.
Yukihira: Something which looks technically flawless can end up becoming meaningless if it appeals to nobody's eyes.
Yukihira: Conversely, something that looks a complete mess can pull in somebody's heart so strongly that it becomes a priceless piece of perfection to them.
Yukihira: In other words...
Yukihira: If you stick to your own style, then it will turn into your best weapon.
Ena: ...!
Ena: If I stick to my own style...
Ena: (That means I'm supposed to draw the way I feel, right...?)
Ena: (But is that really okay? I mean, I...)
Yukihira: This signpost means quite a lot to you, yes?
Ena: Yes!
(Black cut)

Ena: (Hands, huh?)
Ena: (It's a pretty simple theme... But that also means I should be able to look at it from a bunch of different angles.)

Yukihira: If you stick to your own style, then it will turn into your best weapon.

Ena: (Also, how they connect me with everyone else. That matters a lot to me.)
Ena: (And...)
Ena: Oh...
Ena: (Futaba's so fast. She's already begun drawing...)
Ena: (She really is an amazing artist... Even though she's just sketching, she's already glowing.)
Ena: (Still, why does this sensation feel so familiar...?)
Ena: (Oh, that's right... I've seen this scene before.)
Ena: (Back when I had way too much fun drawing.)
